About

I am an Assistant Professor in the Department of Neurology at Johns Hopkins University and the F.M. Kirby Research Center for Functional Brain Imaging at Kennedy Krieger Institute.

My research focuses on brain MRI techniques that measure tissue properties related to neurological diseases, including multiple sclerosis, Parkinson’s disease and dementia. I work in three areas. The first is biophysical modeling, which describes how cellular structures shape the MRI signal through magnetic susceptibility, diffusion and water exchange. These models make it possible to measure properties such as myelin content and iron concentration, which conventional MRI cannot quantify. The second is physics-informed artificial intelligence, which builds physical laws into neural networks to reduce errors and make these measurements fast enough for clinical use. The third is clinical translation, which brings these techniques from the laboratory to the bedside through patient studies with clinical colleagues.

Research

More

Physics-informed deep learning

Neural networks built on MRI physics for fast and reliable quantitative maps.

Iron and myelin imaging

Separate maps of iron and myelin, based on a biophysical model of magnetic susceptibility.

Microstructure MRI

Signals from water in different tissue compartments, used to probe structure below the image resolution.

Clinical applications

Iron and myelin changes in multiple sclerosis, Parkinson's disease and other neurological disorders.
